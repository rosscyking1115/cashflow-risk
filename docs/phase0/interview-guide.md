# Phase 0 interview guide

**Goal:** decide Gate 1 — is the pain real and are current tools insufficient?
**Target:** 5 interviews. **Kill switch:** if fewer than 3 of 5 confirm, stop or
pivot before building further.

## Who to recruit (screening)

A good interviewee is a UK business that:

- invoices other businesses (or is a freelancer) on **payment terms** (14–60 days);
- has **felt** late payments — a chased invoice, a tight month, a delayed payment;
- has revenue concentrated in a **handful of customers** (most B2B service firms do).

Avoid: pure-retail / point-of-sale businesses (paid immediately), and anyone who
only invoices consumers.

## Conduct rules (read before every interview)

- **Don't pitch, don't lead.** You are testing a belief, not selling. If you find
  yourself explaining why it's great, stop.
- **Ask about the past, not the hypothetical.** "Tell me about the last time a
  customer paid late" beats "would you use a tool that…". (The Mom Test.)
- **Let silence do the work.** Let them talk about their real process before you
  show anything.
- **Separate politeness from signal.** "That's cool" is not validation. A specific
  past behaviour, a workaround they built, or asking to keep the mock-up is.

## Structure (~30 minutes)

**1. Warm-up / current process (10 min) — before showing anything**
- Walk me through how you invoice and get paid. What terms?
- Who are your biggest customers by revenue? Roughly what share do the top few make?
- Tell me about the last time a customer paid late. What happened? What did you do?
- How do you decide who to chase, and when? What do you use today (spreadsheet,
  accountant, accounting software, nothing)?
- Have you ever been caught short by a late payment? Walk me through it.

**2. Show the mock-up (5 min)**
- Show the shortfall version of [action-brief-mockup.md](action-brief-mockup.md).
  Say only: "This is generated from a CSV of your invoices." Then be quiet and
  watch where their eyes go and what they ask.

**3. The key question (10 min)**
- **"Would this output change a chasing decision you'd make this week?"** Then dig:
  - Which line would you act on first? Why?
  - What here do you already know, and what's new?
  - What's missing before you'd trust it enough to act?
- What do you use today that does this, and where does it fall short?
- (Only at the very end, for pull signal) Would you want to try it on your own
  invoice export?

**4. Wrap (5 min)**
- Who else should I talk to? Can you introduce me?

## Gate 1 scoring rubric

For each interview, record a yes/no on **both** conditions. An interview *confirms*
only if both are **yes**:

- **Pain is real & current:** they described a specific, recent late-payment episode
  that cost them time, money, or sleep — not a vague "cashflow is hard".
- **Tools are insufficient:** what they use today (spreadsheet / accountant /
  accounting software / nothing) does **not** already tell them which invoice to
  chase this week.

Strong positive signals (note them, they raise confidence): they point to a
specific line they'd act on; they ask to try it on their data; they offer an
introduction.

**Decision:** proceed if **≥ 3 of 5** confirm. Record every interview and the tally
in [interview-log.md](interview-log.md).
